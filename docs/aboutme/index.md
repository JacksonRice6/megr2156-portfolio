#About Me


**Introduction**

My name is Jackson Rice. I am a mechanical engineer at UNC Charlotte in my junior year. About 10 years ago I visited a STEM festival in DC and saw Lockheed Martin there. They were showcasing an exoskeleton suit that they created. I leaned over to my dad and said one day I would work for them. Something about building something that solves a problem just seemed so exciting to me. So since then I have pursued engineering.

As soon as I could I started taking engineering classes. In high school I took three years of Drafting and got certified in AutoCAD and Inventor. I learned critical skills such as how to draw up engineering sketches and how to take it from there into CAD. I learned the importance of clear communication and standards. As I pursued engineering more and more I fell in love with it more. I cannot imagine doing anything else at this point.

When I got to college I did not know which kind of engineering I wanted to do. I took an intro to engineering class, which taught me a little bit of each. I knew it was either going to be electrical or mechanical. Mechanical ended up being what I fell in love with. We did a project for the mechanical section where we had to build a car to drive three balls into a specific location and that was so much fun. On top of that mechanical engineering encapsulates all other engineering, so it was the most logical decision.

I aim to become an engineer that helps society through hard work and critical thinking. I hope to be an engineer that my company can count on and not have to worry about me and is independent, but works well with a team as well.

**What does it mean to defend an engineering decision : and do you currently know how to do it?**

To defend an engineering decision means to explain why it is the correct and best one. It may not always be good, but you have to make people understand it is the best one. However, above all else, you have to be 100% honest. The second you lie is the second people no longer trusts your decision. Currently, I can make an attempt, but I do not think I could properly defend one.
